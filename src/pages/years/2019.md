## Personal timeline



### Early in the year · Another iMac

I had another iMac by early 2019, a beefier one than our family machine. It gave me enough graphics capability for *Battlefront II* and an introduction to *Red Dead Redemption 2*. It served its purpose until the bigger iMac arrived in summer 2020.

### Spring · An iPad I actually kept

Since I hadn’t upgraded my iPhone in 2018 and was still content with the X, I turned my attention to the redesigned iPad Pro. It had everything I wanted. I picked one up in spring 2019, about six months after release and just before my work travel.

With this iPad, I managed something I rarely did with my phones: I held onto it. It stayed with me until fall 2022, and honestly, I should have kept it until spring 2024.

Looking back, I got too caught up in mini-LED and the M-series chips. The later models felt thicker and heavier to me, and the display blooming bothered me. I missed what had made me love the 2018 Pro: thin, light, and long-lasting. For my use, the added power didn’t make up for those trade-offs.

The M4 and M5 generations brought the design closer to what I liked. I’d still love to see what an A20 Pro could do in a 13-inch iPad chassis. That’s the version I find myself imagining: the big canvas, without chasing laptop-class power.

### September · Regretting the iPhone 11

Travel made battery life matter in a different way. The iPhone X was struggling to get through the day, and I talked myself into an iPhone 11: a newer chip, more storage, a bigger screen, and better battery life without paying for the top model.

I knew I was giving up things I liked about the X. I made the trade anyway and almost immediately regretted it. The 11 felt bulky, and I missed the display and finish of the more expensive phone. You don’t always appreciate what has been pared away from a device until you live with the alternative.

I should probably have just replaced the X’s battery.
