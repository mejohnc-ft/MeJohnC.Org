## Early in the year · An iMac with my grandmother

At the beginning of the year, my grandmother and I specced out an iMac. We agreed it was worth investing in as a family computer. I had to make a choice between more memory and more storage, and I chose the Fusion Drive. In hindsight, I wish I had chosen the RAM. I still ended up needing an external SSD for Windows.

The machine served me from 2016 to 2018, then my grandmother and cousins kept using it until 2024. Later, I helped her move to a Mac mini and, eventually, a newer MacBook Air with 16GB of memory. Always help the people who helped you.

I wouldn’t be where I am without my grandmother. She gave me room to find my way in technology, often through her wallet. Most kids don’t get that opportunity, and I won’t take it for granted.

I can look back and see a consumeristic kid chasing the next device. I can also see the exposure and curiosity that led me to places I had only dreamed about. She didn’t know what would become of it, but she trusted me enough to make room for it. I can never fully repay that.

## September · The iPhone 7 Plus

The matte-black iPhone 7 Plus is still among my most-used and best-remembered iPhones. I loved the simpler antenna lines and the return of a proper black finish. Jet Black tempted me, but a mostly caseless life made matte the better choice.

I briefly detoured through an iPhone SE during what I can only describe as a spring bout of consumerism. The 7 Plus persisted. Between 3D Touch, the solid-state home button, and the speaker and camera improvements, there was a lot to enjoy even within a familiar shape.

## October · The Crucible

That fall, I played Governor Danforth in *The Crucible* at Hemet High School. After several years in theatre, the production helped me earn my Thespian membership.

It was a substantial commitment: months of memorization, blocking, and rehearsal. I enjoyed every bit of it. I would do it again if I could.
