## Spring · Graduation

2017 was a reset: graduation, and the iPhone X.

## November · The iPhone X

After the iPhone 6, 6s Plus, and 7 Plus, the X felt like what everything had been leading toward. I loved getting a hardware and software shift at the same time. The gestures, the display, Face ID, and the fluidity between them made it feel like a different kind of device.



<div data-year-photos="product-img-0137"></div>

## Authentication as part of the experience

The way I understand the X is that Face ID and the new navigation belong together. Authentication could happen as part of picking up and using the phone, instead of feeling like a separate deliberate step. To me, that made the rest of the experience possible.

That’s my reading of the design, rather than a claim about how Apple’s team arrived at it. But it has stayed with me: when hardware and software are designed around the same idea, you feel it throughout the product.

## A phone worth keeping

The iPhone X was the first iPhone I kept for two years. It sated me completely. I wasn’t drawn to the next model’s colors or details, and by 2018 I was working and couldn’t easily justify another expensive phone anyway.

For once, I had something I wanted to keep using.
